From my scrapbook

Meet the Pastry Junkie

I’m Erykah Williams, a pastry chef and cake artist from Baltimore, Maryland, the face behind The Pastry Junkie. I’ve been baking since I was five, when I used to bring cakes home from my grandma’s house just so I could decorate them.

Little Erykah

My Grandma Retta and I spent a lot of time baking together. I loved the sweets we made, but I especially loved taking a cake home and deciding what it was going to look like. My mom encouraged me at every stage. First it was Easy-Bake ovens and baking kits. Eventually it was piping bags and cake pans in every shape.

I’d take the cakes home just so I could decorate them.

Me in High School

At Carver Voc-Tech, I studied culinary arts and gravitated toward baking. I’d always loved to draw, and once I started taking cake decorating classes at The Cake and Wedding Cottage, I had a way to bring that into the kitchen. By the time I competed in SkillsUSA, I was already thinking about how to make a career out of something I’d been doing for fun for years.

My First Baking Job

My first professional baking job was at Johns Hopkins Hospital. I worked alongside skilled pastry chefs, decorated hundreds of cupcakes a day, and got to make desserts for hospital events. I’d spent years practicing on my own cakes. Now I had other bakers to learn from and hundreds of chances a day to put what I learned to use.

The Original Pastry Junkie

My sister came up with the name “The Pastry Junkie.” The girl in the logo was inspired by me as a kid: the gap in my teeth, the puff balls, and always having something sweet in my hand.

I went from being the family baker to putting together menus, making cake jars, and taking on all kinds of cakes. I still have the pictures. There’s Minnie Mouse, Pokémon, and a cake I covered in crayons and paint palettes. Looking at those cakes now, I can see how much I was willing to try while I was still figuring things out.

Then I Stopped

I was working a full-time job and trying to build a business at the same time. I burned out. For a while, I put down the piping bag.

What I’m Building

I came back to baking because I still wanted to see my dream come true. I still get excited about figuring out how to pull off a cake someone has in their head. Put that old Minnie Mouse cake next to the silver Sweet 16 cake and you can see how much I’ve learned. They’re both mine, and I’m proud of what it took to get from one to the other.

One day, I want to walk into a grocery store and see The Pastry Junkie on the shelf. And I want another little Black girl to see those puff balls on the package and think, She looks like me.

The Pastry Junkie is more than a bakery to me. I’m grateful to finally be building something that reflects who I am and where I came from.

With love,
Erykah
